# S10 Facilitation: Keep Learning Forever

24 October 2026. Plan for 90 minutes of teaching, up to 120 with discussion, followed by the one-hour ceremony. This is a launch into independent learning, not a final examination or a round of mandatory site demonstrations.

## Shape

| Minutes | Focus |
|---|---|
| 0–10 | Recall what learners can now do; invite two brief repair memories |
| 10–35 | Tell the Chrome-audio story and make the investigation method visible |
| 35–50 | Documentation, AI, and asking people for help |
| 50–60 | Ubuntu/WSL2/VM choices, with a short optional Omarchy glimpse |
| 60–80 | Homelab vision and other project paths |
| 80–90 | Book-as-experiment companion, first small step, personal closing |

With 120 minutes, spend the extra time on questions, a short break, and project discussion—not extra tools or obligatory presentations. No installation workshop: hardware, permissions, and downloads would consume the session.

## Make The Method Visible

Use the instructor's real Chrome-audio incident: other applications had sound, Chrome did not; AI helped identify PulseAudio on that laptop, a muted Chrome channel, and `pulsemixer`. Do not imply every Linux computer uses PulseAudio. The important result is understanding one more layer of the system.

Bring actual redacted conversation excerpts if available. Otherwise retell the incident honestly; do not manufacture a transcript or present a staged response as the original. Pause at each observation: what hypothesis does it support, and what would you test next? A tiny safe quoting experiment is available in the self-study if a live example is useful.

Wireless@SGx and the instructor's own Shenzhen cameras are short supporting stories. Wi-Fi illustrates learning an unfamiliar authentication setup. Cameras illustrate an agent connecting protocol discovery (ONVIF), research, authorized default-credential recovery, and recorder configuration. Do not turn this into credential guessing practice; use only owned/authorized devices, change defaults, and keep access local. Wi-Fi certificate validation is not a setting to disable for convenience.

AI can explain, suggest tests, or give procedures. Do not impose an artificial "hints only" rule. Ask what changed, why it should work, how to verify, and how to undo it. Show that AI suggestions can be wrong and need evidence. Do not project secrets or another person's private data.

## Give A Direction, Not A Distribution Survey

Ubuntu LTS is the default, continuing familiar Debian concepts. Roughly two-thirds of the group use Windows: WSL2 is a useful first environment; a VM provides a complete separate Linux machine. For the Mac third, UTM and a matching Intel/Apple Silicon guest image are the starting point.

Omarchy is a brief adventurous option: keyboard-driven, integrated AI tools, an environment learners can reshape. Show a prepared local screen/video if available rather than attempting installation. Check its current official VM/hardware instructions before demonstrating; do not promise universal compatibility or free access to every AI service. Mention that it is Arch-based without starting a distribution debate. No Fedora/Arch comparison lesson.

## Sell The Homelab Vision

One service first: Jellyfin plays one video. Another device joins, a problem gives a reason to read logs, then backup/restore and one additional service. An old PC or available Pi is a useful dedicated host; a VM works when there is only one computer.

Show several motivations: retro gaming, a game server, own cameras, home automation, photos. Explain that retro gaming is usually a local application, not necessarily a network server. Use permitted media/homebrew examples. Keep initial services on the home network. A Pi's capabilities depend on storage and workload, especially transcoding.

The instructor's homelab can supply one photo or a simple diagram: it grew over years, one need at a time. Do not unpack Kubernetes, Bifrost, LDAP, or a full dependency tree. The vision is "you can grow this," not "you must build mine."

## Book And Closing

The gift is *At the Prompt*, the five-zine Julia Evans anthology described in `graduation/book/README.md`. It is the primary reading resource, not the beginning of a shopping list. Use the book slide at the ceremony handover if the gift should stay a surprise during teaching.

Connect reading to action: pick a page, predict, try, change, observe. No required reading order or completion goal. Do not copy purchased pages into slides, distribute the purchased PDFs, or include them in the follow-up ZIP. Course-owned PDFs are separate.

The central message is **KEEP LEARNING FOREVER**. Persistence includes pauses, reducing a problem, changing an approach, reading docs/code, and asking for help. Learning time differs across people; avoid guilt or a promise that a course/project guarantees employment. Invite a next idea, not a graded written commitment or public personal plan.

## After The Ceremony

Confirm the actual server closure date before announcing it. The post-course email should include a per-learner ZIP of useful `~/scripts` and, where useful, `~/playground` files plus course PDFs. No site/build migration, hosting assignment, or learner `tar`/`scp` lesson.

Review exports for passwords, tokens, keys, private data, and oversized/downloaded material before sending. Use private per-learner delivery, not a cohort-visible directory. Prepare clear starting links for Windows and Mac. Do not collect, shut down, or send anything without the operator's authorization; export tooling and email delivery are separate pending work.
